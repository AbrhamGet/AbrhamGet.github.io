A three-day dare that left 700K followers behind

Creative DirectorTikTokHome appliances2 weeks
48Kfollowers at the start
1Mtarget, hit in week one
700K+kept after
14×account size

The problem

Popular Electronics got dared to buy a retired Ethiopian artist a car, on one condition: its TikTok had to reach one million followers. The account had 48,000, and the internet gave it three days.

What I did

Three days of hype burns off fast, so I stretched it into a two-week campaign. The challenge stayed the main story. Around it, I slotted the brand's actual sales content, fridges, TVs and washing machines, so the people showing up for the drama also saw what the shop sells.

Result

The million came inside the first week. Once things calmed down, the account kept more than 700,000 followers. That's a real audience the brand now sells to every day.

Google Ads for Ethiopian green coffee, sold wholesale in Canada

Google Ads leadSearchPerformance MaxB2B wholesale

MCCmanaged with NabTop and Chesco
B2Broasters, retailers, cafés

The problem

TM Coffee sells raw, unroasted Ethiopian beans in bulk to buyers in Edmonton, Ottawa, Winnipeg and Victoria. It's a narrow B2B audience. Broad keywords pull in people who want a bag of coffee for the house.

What I did

I built the Google Ads account from zero: keyword lists, responsive search ads, a Search campaign for buyers already looking for green beans, and a Performance Max campaign for wider reach. I installed the Google tag, Analytics and Search Console, set up conversion tracking so Smart Bidding had real data, and pointed traffic at a "request a sample" page. I also wrote the SEO copy for tmcoffee.ca and its sister brand, tmpackaging.ca.

Where it is now

The campaigns are live. When early sample requests skewed toward offices, I tightened targeting and messaging toward roasters, retailers and cafés, the buyers TM actually wants.

Making fridges worth following

Account ManagerTikTokInstagramFacebookTelegram1 year
110K+TikTok followers
29Mpost views
1.1Mlikes
156Kshares

The problem

Nobody buys a fridge on a whim. Content that only chases reach brings in people who'll never buy one, and spec sheets never reach anyone new.

What I did

I ran two lanes under one idea, "Ease of Life". The first showed the fridge inside someone's real day, built for reach. The second was detailed reviews answering cooling, capacity and warranty questions for people already comparing models. Viewers from the first lane fed into the second. A New Year's campaign turned that reach into follows.

Result

100,000 net followers in a year across platforms, with 110K+ on TikTok alone.

A mascot people recognise, and a Friday habit

Strategy + scriptsTikTokMetaTelegram1 year
12M+views in a year
146K+followers gained
15Kweekly Telegram players
70Kchannel woken up

The problem

Ethiopian betting brands all look alike in a feed: a logo, a payout figure, a stock photo. On top of that, the brand's 70,000-subscriber Telegram channel was basically silent.

What I did

I created Habtam, a green character in a top hat carrying sacks of cash, and ran every post through him. I wrote the scripts and directed the shoots to keep him consistent on TikTok, Meta and Telegram. For Telegram, I started a Friday competition in the same slot every week, simple enough to enter straight from the notification.

Result

People started recognising Habtam on sight, so the brand stopped having to introduce itself. About a fifth of the Telegram channel now shows up every Friday.

The problem

Strictly Soul is an R&B event series, and the Addis edition needed to sell tickets for a very wide venue. Event content also tends to die the morning after.

What I did

Eleven pre-event pieces to build demand, timed so interest peaked right before the date. On the night, live coverage. Afterward, six more pieces to stretch the event out for people who were there and the ones who wished they had been.

Result

1.19M views at a 3.02% engagement rate, solid for event content, with the post-event pieces keeping it going.
